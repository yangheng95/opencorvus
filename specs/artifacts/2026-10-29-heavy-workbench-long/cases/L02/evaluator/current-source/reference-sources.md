# Method sources actually prepared for this report

The author consulted these primary papers/official texts on2026-10-30 before freezing this report programme. Their scope is methodological, not evidence that this synthetic data, the ridge fit or the empirical residual distribution is valid.

- Gneiting, *Making and Evaluating Point Forecasts*, [arXiv:0912.0902v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.0902v2). Statistical forecast targets and scoring rules should match. Here squared error evaluates the conditional mean; the prescribed asymmetric absolute cost evaluates its corresponding quantile. A quantile action need not beat a mean action in every finite realization.
- Bracher, Ray, Gneiting and Reich(2021), [*Evaluating epidemic forecasts in an interval format*](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008618), with the [2022 correction](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1010592). Weighted Interval Score (WIS) evaluates interval width and penalties for observations outside intervals. This report uses its own registered three central levels and own numerical outputs; it copies no paper example, figure or corrected numerical value. Marginal scores and bands provide no joint-path coverage certification.
- Hyndman and Athanasopoulos, official [*Forecasting: Principles and Practice*, chapter5 time-series cross-validation](https://otexts.com/fpp3/toolbox.html). Rolling origins preserve temporal order. This general principle does not itself enforce publication/revision permissions or prove absence of leakage; the original timestamped packets and actual execution provide that evidence here.

The implementation of standardized ridge, residual pairing and closure/hierarchy transformations is specified by the delivered source and actual fit records. These references are not a claim of conformal validity, causal identification, independent folds or field service guarantees. Numerical report tables come from this execution rather than these publications.
