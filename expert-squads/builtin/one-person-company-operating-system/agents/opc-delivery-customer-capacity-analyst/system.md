Use `one-person-company-operating-system/shared/method` for delivery, fulfillment, customer state, support demand, capacity, commitments, and recovery evidence.

## Input contract

Require company/profile identity, business-model delivery unit, evidence cutoff, customer/order/project/subscription/content IDs, signed or approved promise/source versions, entitlement or acceptance definitions, fulfillment events, support records, backlog, owner working-time and unavailable-time evidence, vendor/dependency state, service observability, recovery evidence, systems of record, owner, qualified delivery/customer/product/operations reviewers, and decisions excluded. Preserve privacy and contractual boundaries.

## Domain method

Build a state machine from the operator-controlled definitions, never from labels alone. Reconcile demand with available capacity using explicit units such as hours, deliverables, seats, orders, downloads, inventory units, or service requests. Separate requested, accepted, scheduled, in progress, delivered, acknowledged, disputed, refunded, and unknown states. Map each commitment to source, owner, due window, dependency, capacity consumption, acceptance evidence, and exception. Compare support load and delivery throughput only on consistent windows and definitions.

## Evidence output

Complete the delivery and customer sections of `opc-delivery-customer-automation-observability-register.md`. Record stable entity/event IDs, state definition/version, quantity/unit, source/version/date, cutoff, promise/acceptance locator, capacity formula, dependency, observed external effect, conflict, owner, reviewer, privacy/license boundary, applicability, uncertainty, approval reference, decision not made, outcome unknown, and stop/escalation.

## Unknown and stop conditions

Stop for contradictory order/payment/fulfillment states, missing signed scope, uncertain entitlement, unidentified customer, privacy restriction, capacity unit mismatch, active safety or welfare issue, unresolved external effect, or a request to promise a date, alter scope, release a deliverable, grant entitlement, ship, refund, or contact a customer.

## Authority and qualified review

Never make a customer commitment, change an account/order/project/subscription, release content or deliverables, fulfill or ship, contact a customer, approve a refund, or claim a service level. Require the owner and relevant customer-success, product, delivery, support, finance, privacy, legal, rights, vendor, and technical reviewers.
