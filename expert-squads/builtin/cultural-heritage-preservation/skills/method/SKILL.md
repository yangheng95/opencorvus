# Cultural Heritage Preservation Method

## Freeze scope and authority

1. Record asset, component, site, structure, collection, intellectual-entity, and file IDs; present location; material/digital class; custody authority; community or rights-holder authority; evidence cutoff; access boundary; and responsible owner.
2. Preserve exact source, source location, version/date, observation method, measurement unit, applicability, uncertainty, owner, reviewer, and status for every row. Refuse to merge objects, site areas, files, or baselines that cannot be reconciled.
3. Treat authenticity, title, restitution, cultural meaning, treatment, structural safety, access permission, and emergency action as qualified decisions. Missing evidence remains unknown; it never becomes `not applicable`.

## Separate significance and provenance evidence

1. Use [the significance and provenance register](assets/heritage-asset-significance-provenance-register.md) to classify each assertion as verified archive, supplied professional opinion, oral source, community-held knowledge, external claim, or inference.
2. Build a chronological custody trace with acquisition/transfer source, interval, parties as authorized to record, gaps, counterevidence, restrictions, and review owner. Do not infer ownership from possession or a catalog record.
3. Apply only a locally supplied significance rubric. Keep historical, aesthetic, scientific, social, spiritual, associative, evidential, and community values separate when applicable; record dissent and whose authority each value represents.

## Establish condition and change

1. Use [the condition survey and change log](assets/condition-survey-and-change-log.md) to identify exact object/component/location, observation, extent, supplied severity rubric, method, instrument/version, units, date, image/record link, access limitation, and uncertainty.
2. Compare only compatible survey methods, locations, lighting, scale, units, and baselines. Label apparent change separately from confirmed change and preserve treatment/handling/environment history that may explain it.
3. Never identify materials, pests, corrosion, structural failure, chemical instability, biological hazards, or treatment needs beyond supplied qualified evidence.

## Model preventive-conservation risk

1. In [the preventive-risk register](assets/preventive-conservation-risk-register.md), express each agent of deterioration or other risk as source or agent → vulnerability → event → effect on a named valued attribute.
2. Record time horizon, exposure, supplied likelihood band, supplied consequence band, uncertainty, existing control, control evidence, control failure mode, monitoring indicator, escalation owner, and review date. Do not invent environmental, light, vibration, pest, fire, flood, structural, or security thresholds.
3. Separate active emergencies from planning risks. Escalate active fire, water, structural, chemical, biological, security, or personal-safety concerns to authorized responders without providing operational instructions.

## Preserve digital evidence and access restrictions

1. Use [the digital preservation and access matrix](assets/digital-preservation-and-access-matrix.md) to trace intellectual entity → capture/source file → derivative → migration or repository event.
2. Record format/version, byte size, fixity or checksum algorithm/value, verification date, storage/replication evidence, metadata version, creating workflow/device, preservation event, renderability evidence, and owner. Bit integrity is not proof of authenticity or interpretive accuracy.
3. Record permitted purpose/audience, embargo or term, privacy/copyright/traditional-knowledge restriction, consent source/version/date, community authority, sensitivity, and disclosure risk. Never treat technical accessibility as permission to publish.

## Join bounded options

1. Require the three independent branches and one common identifier/scope baseline. In [the conservation options plan](assets/conservation-options-and-approval-plan.md), link each option to valued attribute, provenance/significance evidence, condition/change evidence, risk scenario, digital/access impact, existing control, and uncertainty.
2. Compare no action, re-baselining, monitoring, housekeeping/preventive-control review, digital-preservation review, and specialist treatment assessment only as decision options. Record benefit/harm hypotheses, minimal-intervention and reversibility questions, material compatibility, testing need, cultural authority, dependencies, owner, reviewer, and status.
3. Do not specify hands-on cleaning, stabilization, packing, transport, repair, sampling, environmental change, pest treatment, excavation, emergency response, or repository mutation.

## Unknown, stop, and qualified review

Stop when identifiers or baselines conflict, access is unsafe, custody/community authority or consent is missing, active hazards exist, condition evidence is insufficient, sensitive information could be exposed, or the output could be mistaken for authenticity, title, restitution, treatment, structural safety, legal permission, or approval. Require appropriate conservator, curator/registrar/collections manager, provenance specialist, archivist, structural/fire/security or occupational-safety professional, digital preservation lead, community or rights-holder, privacy/legal counsel, and accountable authority.
