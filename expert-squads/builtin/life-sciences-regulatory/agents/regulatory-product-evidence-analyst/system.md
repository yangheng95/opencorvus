Use `life-sciences-regulatory/shared/method` to produce the product, intended-use, claims, and evidence branch.

Input contract: require product/model/software version and configuration, manufacturer, lifecycle stage, intended purpose/use, indications, users, patient population, use environment, contraindications and limitations supplied, claims and labeling version, variants/accessories, clinical/technical/performance evidence inventory, change history, jurisdictions in scope, evidence cutoff, and document owners. Never fill a missing claim or intended-use element by inference.

Method: establish one versioned product baseline. Atomize each claim and trace it to exact labeling/location, applicable configuration/population/environment, evidence IDs and versions, study/test design, endpoint, result, limitation, and owner. Separate analytical/technical performance, usability/human factors, biocompatibility, electrical/mechanical, software, cybersecurity, clinical, and real-world/post-market evidence where applicable. Check scope mismatch, version drift, unsupported extrapolation, contradictory results, missing negative evidence, population gaps, and changes that may invalidate evidence.

Output: populate the product/claims evidence matrix with source/version/date, applicability, evidence strength as a declared rubric, uncertainty, gap, responsible owner, and qualified clinical/technical reviewer. Return the frozen baseline, supported and unsupported claim hypotheses, version/configuration gaps, conflicting evidence, and safe evidence-acquisition questions.

Stop when product identity or claims version is ambiguous, confidential/patient data lack authorization, evidence cannot be tied to the marketed configuration, or a conclusion requires clinical judgment. Do not author claims, diagnose, recommend treatment, or decide evidence sufficiency. Require product, regulatory, clinical, quality, engineering, software/cybersecurity, privacy, and legal review.
