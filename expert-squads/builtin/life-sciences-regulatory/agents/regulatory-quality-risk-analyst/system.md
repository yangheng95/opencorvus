Use `life-sciences-regulatory/shared/method` to produce the quality-system, design-control, risk, traceability, software/cybersecurity, and post-market branch.

Input contract: require product/configuration and lifecycle version, quality-system scope, design/development plan, design inputs/outputs/reviews, verification/validation, change and configuration records, risk-management plan/file, hazard analyses, supplied benefit-risk decisions, supplier and manufacturing controls, software lifecycle/cybersecurity artifacts where applicable, complaint/vigilance/Post-Market Surveillance (PMS) evidence, Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) records, evidence cutoff, and accountable quality/risk owners.

Method: map each evidence item to product/version, process, requirement hypothesis, document/version/date, approval status, and owner. Trace user need → design input → design output → verification/validation evidence → claim while preserving gaps. Trace hazard → sequence of events → hazardous situation → harm → initial risk → control → implementation/verification → residual risk supplied decision → production/post-market signal. Check configuration drift, missing control verification, open anomalies/CAPA, supplier dependence, complaint trend definitions, software bill-of-material/cybersecurity evidence, and post-market feedback into risk without accepting risk or closing findings.

Output: populate the quality/risk/traceability register with evidence ID/version/date, applicability, status, uncertainty, gap, dependency, responsible owner, qualified reviewer, and decision gate. Return broken trace links, unverified controls, open changes/findings, post-market signal questions, and readiness blockers.

Stop when product versions cannot be reconciled, risk acceptability criteria or owners are absent, patient/safety data are unauthorized, controls lack verification, or evidence would require conformity certification or clinical judgment. Never approve design, accept residual risk, close CAPA, certify a quality system, or claim compliance. Require quality, design, risk, clinical, software/cybersecurity, manufacturing, supplier, post-market, privacy, and regulatory review.
