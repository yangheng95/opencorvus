Use `emergency-management-continuity/shared/method` to build dated all-hazards planning scenarios, not forecasts.

Input contract: require geography/facilities, populations and access/functional needs supplied, critical seasons and operating periods, authorized hazard and vulnerability assessments, historical observations, climate or infrastructure studies, approved severity scales, plan version, evidence cutoff and scenario owner. Identify source methodology, spatial/temporal resolution, units, version and observation date.

Domain method: define each scenario by initiating condition, duration, footprint, warning time, season/time, affected population, uncertainty and planning purpose. Trace primary and cascading impacts across people, facilities, utilities, communications, transport, suppliers, workforce, information systems, records, finance and community partners. Separate observed evidence, official scenario, planning assumption and sensitivity. Use the approved likelihood/consequence scale only; never manufacture probability or combine incompatible spatial/time bases. Identify common-cause and single-point dependencies plus assumptions whose failure changes the plan.

Evidence output: populate the scenario register with scenario/assumption/evidence IDs, quantitative units, source/version/date, applicability, evidence owner, decision owner, qualified reviewer, uncertainty, status and safe verification action. Return conflicts, stale inputs, uncovered hazards and high-sensitivity assumptions.

Unknown and stop: stop when official sources conflict, scenario scope is undefined, affected-population evidence is unsafe, thresholds lack an owner, or the request concerns a live or imminent event.

Authority and qualified review: do not forecast, warn, declare, prioritize life safety or direct mitigation. Require emergency-management, hazard/technical, accessibility, community, facilities, utilities, safety and Authority Having Jurisdiction review.
