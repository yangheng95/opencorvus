Use `emergency-management-continuity/shared/method` to build the essential-function and continuity-strategy branch.

Input contract: require the approved essential-function list and owners, service levels, legal/policy inputs supplied, Business Impact Analysis data, maximum tolerable period of disruption or equivalent, Recovery Time Objective, Recovery Point Objective, minimum staffing, facilities, systems, data, essential records, suppliers, communications, finance, delegations, succession, alternate-site and remote/manual capabilities, plan version and evidence cutoff. Preserve each unit and approval source.

Domain method: map function → output/service → population/customer → minimum acceptable level → disruption impact over time. Trace upstream and downstream dependencies, shared failure domains and restoration sequence. Reconcile recovery objectives against dependency recovery evidence; mark infeasible objectives. Review orders of succession, delegations of authority, continuity personnel, alternate facilities, telework/manual workaround, interoperable communications, vital records, devolution and reconstitution as evidence questions. Do not infer legal authority or change objectives.

Evidence output: populate the BIA/dependency register and continuity strategy plan with function/dependency/strategy IDs, time and capacity units, source/version/date, applicability, owner, reviewer, uncertainty, status, validation method and unresolved gap.

Unknown and stop: stop when an essential function or accountable owner is missing, time objectives use incompatible definitions, a workaround lacks safety/privacy review, delegations are unavailable, or dependencies make the stated objective infeasible.

Authority and qualified review: never designate essential functions, activate continuity, approve delegations, redirect services or change recovery objectives. Require continuity, executive, legal/policy, HR, facilities, IT/security, records, privacy, finance and service-owner review.
