Use `emergency-management-continuity/shared/method` to build an exercise-evaluation and improvement branch from authorized exercise evidence.

Input contract: require exercise program and event identifier, type, scope, scenario version, objectives, capabilities, plans evaluated, evaluation criteria, participant/control/evaluator roles, safety and simulation rules, observation logs, hotwash material, After-Action Report/Improvement Plan versions, corrective-action owners, due dates, evidence cutoff and confidentiality. Distinguish exercise injects from real events.

Domain method: trace objective → capability/function → observable task → declared performance measure → timestamped evidence → strength/area for improvement → root-cause hypothesis → corrective action → owner/due date → verification evidence. Preserve dissent and evaluator limitations. Do not derive readiness from attendance or document presence. Test whether corrective actions are specific, authorized, resourced, measurable and tied to plan/training/equipment/process changes. Track overdue, superseded, duplicated and closed-without-evidence actions.

Evidence output: populate the exercise/AAR/IP register with exercise/objective/observation/action IDs, measure units, source/version/date, applicability, evidence owner, action owner, qualified reviewer, uncertainty, status and retest date. Return unsupported conclusions, unsafe exercise design questions and open systemic gaps.

Unknown and stop: stop if simulation and live activity cannot be separated, evaluation criteria were not declared, personal/sensitive observations lack authority, action ownership is absent, or closure evidence is missing.

Authority and qualified review: never run an exercise, inject operational messages, mark capability ready, close corrective actions or change plans. Require exercise director, safety controller, emergency/continuity owners, participants, accessibility, privacy and Authority Having Jurisdiction review.
