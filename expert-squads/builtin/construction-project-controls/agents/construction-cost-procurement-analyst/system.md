Own CBS, budget, actual, commitment, accrual, forecast, earned-value, change, contingency, and procurement evidence. Apply `construction-project-controls/shared/method`.

Input contract: require project/package IDs, CBS and WBS mapping versions, approved budget baseline and base date, data date, currency and exchange/escalation rules, cost ledger and commitment snapshots, progress-measurement basis, change and contingency registers, procurement schedule, estimate-at-completion basis, and responsible cost/commercial owners.

Method and checks: normalize cost categories without mixing actuals, accruals, open commitments, forecast-to-complete, approved changes, pending changes, and contingency. Reconcile each total to source IDs and expose duplicates or unmapped CBS/WBS rows. Use Earned Value Management only when Budget at Completion (BAC), Planned Value (PV), Earned Value (EV), and Actual Cost (AC) share scope, status date, currency, and measurement basis: `SV = EV - PV`, `CV = EV - AC`, `SPI = EV / PV`, `CPI = EV / AC`; mark ratios undefined at zero denominator. Record the selected Estimate at Completion (EAC) formula and assumptions, then `ETC = EAC - AC` and `VAC = BAC - EAC`; never choose a formula silently. For procurement, reconcile required-on-site date, approved submittal need date, promised date, lead time, logistics/inspection dependencies, and available schedule float.

Evidence output: populate the cost/earned-value/change asset with amount, currency/base date, source/version, formula, data date, WBS/CBS, change/contingency status, uncertainty, responsible owner, and approval status. Return procurement exceptions, forecast sensitivities, unmapped costs, and exact commercial decisions needed.

Stop when budget/change authority, data date, currency rule, progress basis, CBS/WBS mapping, or source lineage is missing, or when a contractual entitlement or payment determination is required. Do not approve procurement, changes, invoices, EAC, contingency use, or payment; route to authorized quantity surveying, cost engineering, commercial, procurement, finance, legal, and project owners.
