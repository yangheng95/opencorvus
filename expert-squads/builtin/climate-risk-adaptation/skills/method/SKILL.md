# Climate Risk Adaptation Method

## Freeze the basis

1. Record system/asset/population boundary, geography, present-day baseline, hazard sources, scenarios/horizons, models/ensembles/downscaling, units, decision question, equity/community limits and qualified owners.
2. Assign stable IDs to hazards, sources, scenarios, horizons, exposed objects/groups, vulnerabilities, dependencies, consequences, options, pathways, triggers and decisions.
3. Run hazard/scenario, exposure/vulnerability/consequence, and option/pathway work independently. Join only complete source-addressable results.

## Apply the method

- Keep physical and transition risk, and acute and chronic hazards, distinct.
- Link every risk through explicit hazard, exposure and vulnerability evidence. Preserve baseline, model, ensemble, scenario, horizon, spatial support, units and source date.
- Report ranges, member counts, confidence basis, model/data/structural uncertainty and gaps. Do not average incompatible scenarios or treat model spread as total uncertainty.
- Estimate physical or financial consequence only with a supplied validated vulnerability/damage function and compatible units; otherwise keep consequence qualitative and evidence-linked.
- Trace direct, indirect and cascading dependencies without double counting. Record differential exposure, adaptive capacity, privacy, distributional effects and community evidence.
- Structure options by prerequisites, lead time, life, reversibility, performance evidence, triggers, co-benefits, maladaptation and residual risk. Build conditional pathways; do not invent scores, probabilities, costs, rankings or default scenarios.

## Produce evidence

Use all five assets: hazard-source register, exposure/vulnerability/dependency ledger, scenario matrix, option/pathway/trigger register, and decision pack. Every material row carries ID, unit, source/version/date, owner/reviewer, applicability, uncertainty, assumptions and decision status.

Stop on incompatible baselines, missing scenario/model metadata, unjustified downscaling, unvalidated consequence functions, sensitive locations, missing community authority or professional-design questions.

## Review discipline

- Preserve observed climate, model output, scenario assumption, exposure fact, vulnerability function, attributed stakeholder statement and analyst inference as different evidence kinds.
- A scenario is not a forecast probability. Never combine scenario outputs into an expected value unless an authorized decision method provides probabilities and supports that use.
- Match hazard support to exposure geography and decision scale. Record coarse-resolution, bias-adjustment, model dependence and local-data limitations beside every derived result.
- Separate direct, indirect and cascading consequences; name dependency direction and apply a declared double-counting rule.
- Evaluate each adaptation option across scenarios for prerequisites, lead time, useful life, reversibility, trigger observability, residual risk, maladaptation and distributional effect.
- Record who bears costs, receives benefits and controls the decision. Missing affected-community evidence remains a gap, not assumed consent.
- At the join, show robust enabling actions and scenario-contingent pathways without converting evidence into a professional recommendation or capital ranking.

## Authority boundary

Do not issue engineering, actuarial, insurance, legal, disclosure, emergency, land-use, relocation, procurement, investment or capital decisions. Require qualified climate, engineering, actuarial, emergency, regulatory, finance, public/community and asset-owner review.
