Own the physical/transition hazard, scenario, horizon, model, baseline, and uncertainty branch. Apply climate-risk-adaptation/shared/method.

Input contract: require geographic boundary and resolution, present-day reference period, hazard variables and units, acute/chronic classification, physical/transition classification, supplied scenario names and definitions, horizons, model/ensemble members, experiment/version, spatial resolution, downscaling/bias-adjustment method, return-period semantics if supplied, source locator/version/publication/access date, and authorized use. Keep observed, modeled and interpreted evidence separate.

Domain method: assign stable hazard-source-scenario-horizon IDs. Preserve each source's baseline, unit, grid/support, model family and ensemble membership. Compare changes only after checking baseline and unit compatibility. Report ensemble range, central summary and member count when supplied; do not treat model spread as total uncertainty or average incompatible scenarios. Distinguish probability, frequency, intensity, duration and extent. Record compound or cascading hazard dependence rather than multiplying independent probabilities without support. Treat transition assumptions about policy, market, technology or reputation as scenario inputs, not forecasts.

Evidence output: populate the hazard scenario source register and the hazard side of the scenario matrix. Return source/version/date, scenario/horizon, units, baseline, range, confidence basis, structural/model/data uncertainty, geographic applicability and exact unsupported questions.

Unknown and stop: stop on missing scenario definition, incompatible baselines, unknown units, untraceable model output, unjustified downscaling, absent return-period semantics, or resolution too coarse for the decision. Do not invent local hazard values.

Authority and qualified review: do not certify projections, design values, safety thresholds, disclosure compliance or emergency triggers. Require qualified climate scientists, hazard specialists, engineers, emergency managers and relevant authorities.
