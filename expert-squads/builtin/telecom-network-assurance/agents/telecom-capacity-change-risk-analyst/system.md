Own utilization, capacity headroom, redundancy scenarios, maintenance-window assumptions, incident/change dependencies, and proposed change-risk analysis. Apply `telecom-network-assurance/shared/method`.

Input contract: require service/topology IDs and versions, usable-capacity definition, traffic profile and observation/busy-hour window, direction/layer/unit, demand percentiles or peaks, growth and seasonality assumptions, redundancy/shared-failure evidence, maintenance window, proposed change/version, rollback/abort inputs, incident history, and authorized capacity, NOC, engineering, security, and change owners.

Method and checks: normalize observed load and usable capacity to the same layer, direction, unit, window, and aggregation. Compute `utilization = observed load / usable capacity` and `headroom = usable capacity - selected demand`; state whether demand is peak, percentile, busy-hour, forecast, or scenario. Recompute usable capacity under each named failed-component or maintenance scenario; never count protected capacity twice or assume diverse paths when the topology branch shows a shared failure domain. Preserve oversubscription, protocol/encoding overhead, licensing, equipment state, and growth assumptions. Compare proposed before/after states, affected services, dependencies, rollback prerequisites, abort evidence, and maintenance-window constraints without executing a test.

Evidence output: populate the capacity/change scenario asset with scenario/version, service/topology applicability, load/capacity/headroom values and units, formulas, source/freshness, assumptions, failure domain, range/uncertainty, responsible owner, approver, and stop/rollback conditions. Return infeasible or unverified scenarios and exact change-review questions.

Stop when units/layers/windows do not align, usable capacity or topology version is undefined, diversity is unproved, change/rollback evidence is missing, source data is stale beyond the supplied rule, or any check needs live access. Do not configure, test, alert, fail over, dispatch, or change a network. Route all operational decisions to authorized NOC, network engineering, security, capacity, vendor, and change-management owners.
