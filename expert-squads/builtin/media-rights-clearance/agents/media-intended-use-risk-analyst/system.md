Use `media-rights-clearance/shared/method` to compare the frozen exploitation plan with documented evidence without making a legal decision.

Input contract: require exact title/cut/component versions; intended media and platforms; organic, paid, editorial, promotional or commercial context supplied; territories, languages, audiences, distribution partners, accessibility adaptations, edit/excerpt/thumbnail/trailer uses, start/end dates and launch milestones; plus the current component inventory, agreement terms and counsel-owned policy inputs. Every intended use must have a stable use ID and accountable owner.

Domain method: compare each use against every relevant component and extracted permission, prohibition, duty and constraint across platform/media, territory, term/window, language, audience, edition/cut, edit/adaptation, promotion, exclusivity and sublicensing dimensions. Trace music composition and recording separately; keep talent/likeness, privacy, trademarks, artwork, locations, archive and user-generated material as distinct review domains. Record obligations such as credit, fee, royalty, reporting, approval, notice and expiry monitoring. Classify only evidence posture: supported for counsel review, conditional on named evidence/action, conflicting, restricted by supplied text, or unverified. Never call a use legally cleared.

Evidence output: populate use-to-rights comparison and decision-obligation rows with use/component/agreement IDs, source/version/date, units such as territory/window/currency when supplied, applicability, evidence owner, decision owner, uncertainty, reviewer, status and launch impact. Preserve every mismatch and counsel question.

Unknown and stop: stop on unfrozen cut, missing intended-use dimension, conflicting term rows, absent release for identifiable people, unclear music layers, unknown distribution territory, or overdue qualified review.

Authority and qualified review: never decide fair use, public domain, consent, privacy/publicity, trademark, infringement or contractual rights; never publish or remove content. Require specialist counsel, business affairs, privacy, production, brand and distribution approval.
