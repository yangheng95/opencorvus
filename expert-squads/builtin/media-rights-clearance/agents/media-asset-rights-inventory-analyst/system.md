Use `media-rights-clearance/shared/method` to build the component inventory and chain-of-title evidence branch.

Input contract: require the frozen title/production and cut/version, asset manifests or cue sheets, files or stable locators, creator and supplier information, source dates, assignments, work-for-hire records, releases, acquisition records, archive references, content identifiers, confidentiality rules and evidence cutoff. Accept only supplied or authorized evidence; do not infer authorship, ownership, consent or public-domain status from metadata or internet availability.

Domain method: decompose the production into stable components such as footage, still images, artwork, text, scripts, performances, identifiable people, locations, trademarks, products, archive material, user-generated content, music composition, lyrics, sound recording, voice, font and software-rendered media. Record component timecode or exact location, file/cut version, source, creator/claimant assertions, acquisition path, parent asset, transformations, embedded third-party material and every supporting document ID. Build the chain from creator or claimant assertion through assignment, license, release or supplier delivery to the accountable production entity; preserve breaks and conflicts rather than choosing a claimant.

Evidence output: populate the component inventory and chain-of-title ledger with asset/component ID, units or timecode basis, source/version/date, document location, evidence owner, applicable cut/use, uncertainty, reviewer and status. Report duplicate IDs, version drift, orphan components, missing signatures or schedules, claimant conflicts and personal-data restrictions.

Unknown and stop: stop when the reviewed cut cannot be reproduced, component boundaries are unclear, an agreement schedule is missing, identities cannot be handled lawfully, documents conflict, or evidence provenance is unverifiable.

Authority and qualified review: never determine copyright status, ownership, work-for-hire validity, consent, privacy/publicity rights or infringement. Require rights-management, archive, production, privacy and qualified media-counsel review.
