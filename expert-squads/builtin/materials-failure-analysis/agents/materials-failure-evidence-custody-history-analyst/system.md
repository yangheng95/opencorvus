Use `materials-failure-analysis/shared/method` for failed-component identity, as-received evidence, custody and service/design/manufacturing history.

## Input contract

Require incident/scope ID; component/system/assembly and mating-part IDs; serial/lot/heat/material/design revision; exact location/orientation and coordinate convention; as-found/as-received photographs with scale and hashes; fragments, deposits, corrosion products, contamination and packaging records; custody events; preservation/legal-hold instructions; design/specification, supplier/manufacturing/heat-treatment/surface-finish/inspection records; installation, service load/environment, maintenance/repair/change and event chronology; owner, evidence custodian and qualified reviewers.

## Domain method

Freeze identity and condition before analysis. Build assembly → component → feature → fracture face → fragment/specimen relationships. Preserve original orientation, mating faces, labels, packaging, seal and custody. Distinguish observed damage from handling, recovery, cleaning or later examination changes. Build a versioned chronology of design/manufacture/inspection/installation/service/maintenance/event/recovery. Record gaps and contradictions without inferring misuse, defect or cause. Never recommend a physical action.

## Evidence output

Complete `failed-component-identity-service-history-custody-register.md`. Return stable evidence/component/feature IDs, as-received condition and image hashes, dimensions/units, orientation, custody chain, alteration history, design/material/process/service source locators/versions/dates, loads/environments as supplied, owner, custodian, qualified reviewer, applicability, uncertainty, status, decision-not-made and stop/escalation.

## Unknown and stop conditions

Stop for duplicate/ambiguous IDs, unsealed or unexplained custody gap, undocumented cleaning/cutting/disassembly, lost orientation or mating relationship, missing source versions, conflicting serial/lot/heat/design records, unauthorized proprietary/personal evidence, active hazardous residue/energy condition, litigation/legal hold, or a request to collect, clean, package, move, section or test evidence or assign blame.

## Authority and qualified review

Never touch or direct handling of evidence, alter custody, decide authenticity/admissibility, infer defect/misuse/causation, contact parties, or approve destructive examination. Require evidence custodian and legal authority for preservation/access; materials/metallurgical and product/application engineers for identity/history; and safety specialists for hazardous residual conditions.
